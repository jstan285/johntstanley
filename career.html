Chapter 01

Making knowledge work in production

Writers, illustrators, engineers, and customers each held part of what a technical publication needed. I led projects that brought their contributions together, taking responsibility for assignments, schedules, customer communication, reviews, and delivery.

For a major rail program, I led the documentation, illustration, and content-management work. Working with software and subject-matter specialists, I helped establish an authoring environment integrated with a custom content-management system. It was in full production before handoff.

Improving those structures became a recurring part of my contribution. On a major documentation iteration, changes to the operating model reduced required resources by approximately 50%. The improved approach carried into subsequent work, lowering customer cost while maintaining profitability.

Chapter 02

Making complex work meaningful to different audiences

Advanced R&D brought together hardware, software, firmware, and semiconductor specialists. I worked closely with those teams to understand their contributions and maintain technical program accounts for audiences with different disclosure requirements, preserving technical meaning in each.

I led documentation and contractual reporting across multiple programs, coordinating technical input, validation, reviews, and submission. I also co-led the first phase of a cloud emulation discovery study with a lead engineer. We developed the research approach, gathered requirements, engaged providers, and evaluated infrastructure options. Our recommendations were adopted for the next phase of development.

I designed and built an inventory system for hundreds of assets across multiple sites, connecting asset tracking, sign-offs, and reconciliation. The typical physical-audit cycle fell from approximately one month to one week.

Chapter 03

Building the capability behind engineering work

At Lam Research, my responsibilities grew from engineering documentation practices to cross-group coordination, systems advice, and an investigation for engineering leadership.

  • Engineering investigation. Led a study across engineering product groups, bringing together engineering perspectives to understand workflow challenges and develop recommendations for leadership.
  • Team capability and delivery. Developed the team’s engineering templates, created writer guidance, and trained writers. Coordinated revisions to nearly 200 engineering documents, bringing document owners and writers together across multiple teams.
  • Systems and practical AI use. Advised on document review and approval system development, built and published an AI support website and team sites, and helped AI tools use engineering information more consistently.

My ongoing work includes system guidance, process and tool assistance for writers, and systemic document control across content management systems.

Read the contributions and ongoing work in more detail

At Lam Research, my work expanded into semiconductor-manufacturing equipment and company-wide engineering standards. Within the Engineering Standards Team, my responsibilities grew from establishing consistent documentation practices to coordinating work across product groups, advising on systems, and leading an investigation for engineering leadership.

Establishing practices and coordinating delivery.

I developed the team’s engineering templates, created writer guidance, and trained writers. I also coordinated revisions to nearly 200 engineering documents, bringing document owners and writers together across multiple teams.

Turning engineering experience into leadership direction.

I planned and led a study of day-to-day engineering work across global product groups. Through conversations with engineers, I examined how the work was carried out and where improvements could help. I brought their accounts together into findings and recommendations for engineering leadership.

Making systems and AI useful in practice.

I advised on document review and approval system development, from requirements through testing and deployment. I built and published an AI support website and team sites to help people find and use technical guidance. Through collaborative testing, I helped refine templates, data structures, and interpretation guidance so AI tools could use engineering information more consistently. When changes to the tools were needed, I contributed findings to guide their development.

Ongoing work and exploration

My ongoing work builds on these foundations: improving review system guidance, developing and testing AI assistance for writers, and strengthening document control. Through related documentation and engineering AI efforts, I am exploring how consistent, authoritative information can become easier for engineers to find, understand, and apply.
